# Mastermind Brand & Product Architecture — Three-Loop Redo

**Date:** 2026-08-25  
**Scope:** company identity, first-product identity, public module naming, collision risk, and pre-product positioning for the repository currently named `The-Gauntlet`  
**Decision class:** working pre-product identity, not legal clearance

## Decision

**Working company identity:** **Strong Inference Systems**  
**First open product line:** **Strong Inference**  
**Descriptor:** **Open Research Suite**  
**Release frame:** **free pre-product preview**

This is a complete redo of the rejected `Rigilum / Instrument 01` proposal. The rejected names have no continuing status.

The company and product use the same scientific root deliberately. `Strong inference` is an established scientific-method concept, associated with John R. Platt's 1964 formulation: devise alternative hypotheses, design a crucial or discriminating experiment with outcomes that eliminate alternatives, execute for a clean result, and iterate on what survives. The phrase is not claimed here as a newly invented scientific idea.

The fit is unusually direct for this software family: the suite repeatedly branches explanations, searches for counterexamples and prior art, chooses claim-native verifiers, designs matched comparisons, preserves negative evidence, and keeps unresolved state explicit rather than treating agreement as proof.

This report does **not** establish trademark availability, corporate-name availability, domain availability, registrability, or freedom to operate. The exact-name collision screen is only an engineering/branding filter before formal clearance.

## Loop 1 — Meaning and architecture

### Objective

Construct a company/product architecture from the actual intellectual character of the software rather than from invented startup vocabulary.

### Hard constraints

This loop banned:

- pseudo-Latin or biotech-sounding invented words;
- acronym-first names such as `S3`;
- names that require an etymology lesson to sound credible;
- generic `AI + noun` naming;
- forcing the company and every product/module into one repeated prefix;
- renaming stable runtime IDs merely to make marketing vocabulary uniform.

### What the company must be able to become

The company identity must remain credible if the work expands from research software into:

- mathematical and scientific computing;
- automated experimentation;
- simulation;
- research infrastructure;
- autonomous research systems;
- eventually physical scientific systems or laboratory tooling.

That ruled out names tied only to LLMs, benchmarking, verification, or one present-day repository.

### Naming territories explored

The useful branches came from real ideas rather than invented sounds:

1. **scientific reference / measurement** — reference frame, standard candle, known-good state;
2. **proof / falsification** — first proof, counterexample, falsifiable systems;
3. **hard-problem / engineering** — hard problems, scientific machine works;
4. **scientific method** — strong inference.

### Loop 1 finalists

The strongest conceptual finalists were:

- **Strong Inference Systems / Strong Inference**;
- **Falsifiable Systems / Falsifiable**;
- **Reference Frame** as a company-facing candidate;
- **Scientific Machine Works** as an engineering-institution candidate;
- the incumbent **The Gauntlet** as a product candidate that had to be beaten rather than assumed disposable.

**Loop 1 result:** advance real scientific concepts and institutional language; no invented-word candidate survives simply because it is unique.

## Loop 2 — Collision and adversarial kill screen

### Objective

Attack the Loop 1 candidates with current company, software, research-project, package, and trademark searches. A strong semantic fit does not excuse a live adjacent collision.

### Killed candidates

The screen eliminated multiple otherwise attractive names:

- **Reference Frame** — active company usage surfaced; the term is also broadly established technical vocabulary.
- **Scientific Machine Works** — active/historical machine-company usage surfaced.
- **Standard Candle** — active software-company usage surfaced.
- **Known Good** — active software-company usage surfaced.
- **Working Theory** — active AI/software consulting usage surfaced.
- **First Proof** — now the name of a 2026 research-level mathematics AI benchmark/project.
- **Noether** variants — active software/AI firms occupy the territory.
- **Proof Load / Proofload** — active industrial inspection/testing companies use the name.
- **Hard Problems** — an active UK nonprofit registered in 2024 already operates at `hardproblems.com` and targets designers/technology workers tackling climate, public-health, and government problems.
- **Crux** — heavily occupied across software/data/scientific contexts.

### The incumbent product name also failed

The redo did not grandfather **The Gauntlet**.

By 2026, Basalt Labs operates **Gauntlet**, a community-driven behavioral research/evaluation platform for large language models with deterministic probes, benchmarking, leaderboards, MCP, and CI/CD. That is directly adjacent to this repository's AI evaluation/research-software space. Separately, Oak Ridge National Laboratory published scientific software named **Gauntlet** in 2025 for building-morphology feature generation.

Key sources:

- Basalt Labs Gauntlet repository: https://github.com/Basaltlabs-app/Gauntlet
- Basalt Labs Gauntlet product: https://www.basaltlabs.app/gauntlet
- `gauntlet-cli` on PyPI: https://pypi.org/project/gauntlet-cli/
- Oak Ridge National Laboratory Gauntlet software record: https://impact.ornl.gov/en/publications/gauntlet/
- Hard Problems organization: https://www.hardproblems.com/jobs
- First Proof research project: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05192

**Disposition:** `The-Gauntlet` remains the repository slug and `/gauntlet` remains Assure's stable command for compatibility, but **The Gauntlet is no longer the preferred public product brand**.

### Strong Inference screen

Current exact-phrase web searches for **Strong Inference Systems** did not surface an operating software/science company using that exact company name. Searches for **Strong Inference** are dominated by the established scientific-method phrase and papers discussing it rather than a same-name research-software vendor.

That is a scoped `NOT FOUND WITHIN SEARCH`, not legal availability.

The principal live weakness is semantic ambiguity in modern AI: `inference` also means model-serving/execution. The product therefore needs an explicit scientific-method descriptor and should not be marketed as an `inference platform` without qualification.

Scientific-method references:

- Platt concept overview and original citation trail: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_inference
- Beard & Kushmerick, *Strong Inference for Systems Biology*: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2724742/

**Loop 2 result:** Strong Inference Systems survives the current collision screen; the prior public product name does not.

## Loop 3 — External corporate-science comparator and synthesis

### Objective

Compare the surviving architecture against a concrete cohort rather than judging names by internal taste alone.

### Comparator

The primary cohort is the **Nature Index 2026 Research Leaders: Leading corporate institutions**, based on Nature Index data from 1 January through 31 December 2025.

Source: https://www.nature.com/nature-index/research-leaders/2026/institution/corporate/all/global

Nature Index is used only as a reproducible set of research-active companies. Its publication metric is not treated as a comprehensive company-quality or branding ranking.

The cohort includes compact institutional brands such as **Roche, AstraZeneca, Alphabet, IBM, Pfizer, Lilly, Novartis, BGI, AbbVie, NTT, GSK, Microsoft, Meta, QuEra, and Xanadu**, alongside longer legal/descriptive names. The relevant lesson is not "all scientific companies need one-word names." It is that serious research organizations usually present a memorable institutional identity and let products, programs, laboratories, or platforms carry their own functional names.

The Nature & Science corporate subset reinforces the same pattern with Alphabet, Microsoft, Meta, Roche, QuEra, Xanadu, BGI, and others.

Sources:

- Global corporate cohort: https://www.nature.com/nature-index/research-leaders/2026/institution/corporate/all/global
- Nature & Science corporate cohort: https://www.nature.com/nature-index/research-leaders/2026/institution/corporate/nature-science/global

Why Strong Inference wins:

- it contains a real scientific doctrine rather than simulated scientific tone;
- the doctrine maps to the suite's actual branching/falsification/verification behavior;
- it can extend beyond LLM software into broader scientific systems;
- it does not require an acronym;
- the company form `Strong Inference Systems` distinguishes the institution from the product line without inventing another brand;
- its weakness — AI-serving ambiguity around the word `inference` — can be handled by disciplined descriptors, whereas a direct same-market name collision cannot.

**Loop 3 result:** select **Strong Inference Systems → Strong Inference** as the working architecture.

## Positioning

**Company:** Strong Inference Systems  
**Company idea:** scientific systems for reasoning, evidence, experimentation, and discovery  
**First product:** Strong Inference  
**Descriptor:** Open Research Suite  
**Launch state:** pre-product preview  
**Price:** free, MIT licensed  
**Primary product promise:** coordinate technical work around competing explanations and the evidence obligation rather than presenting one undifferentiated assistant  
**Trust strategy:** source + mechanical validation + reproducibility + explicit research boundary + preserved null/negative evidence

Avoid using `SIS` as the public brand. Avoid describing the company merely as an `AI inference company`, which would point users toward model-serving infrastructure rather than the scientific-method meaning.

## Gates before a real corporate launch

2. British Columbia and/or federal corporate-name search and reservation strategy.
3. Domain and social-handle acquisition strategy; do not assume the obvious `stronginference.com` is available.
4. Counsel review before commercialization or filing.
5. GitHub organization creation and repository-transfer plan only after the identity is accepted.
6. Package names, Pages URLs, redirects, release links, signing, CI secrets, and dependency references audited before a repository move.
7. Only after those gates: remove the `working identity` qualifier and consider repository/package namespace migration.
